The context / Enterprise energy · B2B

Real infrastructure.
Real responsibility.

An inspection platform for large energy companies, connecting aerial imagery of power lines and poles with asset data, AI detections and expert assessment.

A missed defect is more than a row in a table. Inspection decisions inform work that protects people, keeps electricity flowing and reduces the risk of equipment failure and fires. The reviewer brings professional judgment and accountability to that decision.

Who uses it

Specialists working as one team.

Administrators set up access and assets. Uploaders bring in imagery; associators connect it to the grid. Reviewers assess the evidence, while CEOs and project leaders monitor the wider picture.

The scale of the work

Tens of thousands of images.

A single upload can contain tens of thousands of images. This data-heavy workflow needs clear processing states, focused queues and a traceable path from each image to a decision.

Human in the loop

AI proposes. The human decides.

AI helps surface potential defects. A qualified reviewer accepts, rejects or records an assessment. Human judgment remains authoritative; a later AI run must not overwrite it.

Exit criterionEveryone describes the core functionality the same way.

Cheap to be wrong

Experiments are disposable wireframes and HTML sketches. They carry no production-code commitment; rejecting one costs hours rather than a build cycle.

The internal loop absorbs the noise

The team tests weak variants before the client review. I bring forward the options that are useful to react to.

Feedback on something tangible

The client can react to a screen and its behavior. Ambiguity surfaces during exploration, while a change is still inexpensive.

Understanding is the exit criterion

The loop ends when the team and client can describe the core functionality consistently. The specification records that agreement.

What the workshops taught me

Seeing the rough whole often expanded the idea set before it narrowed. The client could finally see what was possible—which is why the review artifact had to stay cheap to change.
